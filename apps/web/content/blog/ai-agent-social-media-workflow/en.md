Claude, ChatGPT or a coding agent can write a post, adapt it for each network and suggest a time slot. What they can’t know is what your brand can afford to say. The right setup is to hand them the repetitive work and keep the decision.

> [!NOTE]
> Postbiz doesn’t offer agent access yet: the MCP server, the command line and the public API are announced as “coming soon”. The method below works with any tool. Today, you take the agent’s output and schedule it in the calendar.

## What you delegate, what you keep

| You delegate | You keep |
|---|---|
| First drafts and per-network variants | Brand voice and final approval |
| Splitting a long piece into several posts | Sensitive topics and unhappy customers |
| Suggesting publishing slots | Choosing the slot |
| Rephrasing, shortening, translating | Anything that commits the brand: prices, promises, figures |

## The five-step workflow

1. **Give a brief.** Audience, tone, this week’s goal, limits. A precise brief is worth ten rounds of edits (see [how to write one](/blog/brief-for-ai-agent)).
2. **Ask for drafts, not publications.** The agent prepares; it doesn’t publish.
3. **Review in a batch.** One review session a week beats ten isolated approvals.
4. **Schedule from the calendar.** You pick the slot, and you see the whole week before it goes out.
5. **Look at what worked.** The posts that did well become the examples in the next brief.

## Why drafts rather than direct publishing

A published post can’t really be taken back: even deleted, it may have been seen, copied or screenshotted. An agent can get the tone wrong, invent a figure or answer off-topic. Keeping a human step between the text and the publication costs a few minutes; removing it will, one day, cost much more.

That’s also why the Postbiz sending engine [never replays a publication whose outcome is uncertain](/blog/never-replay-uncertain-publication): automating shouldn’t mean publishing twice.

> [!TIP]
> Ask the agent to flag what it couldn’t verify (a price, a date, a quote). You’ll review those points first.

## Getting started

Pick a single network and a single week. Write the brief, ask for five drafts, review them, schedule them. After one week you’ll know what is worth delegating, and what isn’t.
