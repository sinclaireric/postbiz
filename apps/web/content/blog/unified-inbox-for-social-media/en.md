A customer comments on your Instagram post, another writes to you on Messenger, a third asks a question under your YouTube video. Each network has its own inbox, its own notifications, its own habits. The result: you reply where you happen to look, and forget the rest.

## The problem: as many inboxes as networks

When messages are scattered, three things get worse:

- **speed**: you spend your time switching apps;
- **ownership**: nobody knows who has already answered what;
- **memory**: a customer’s history is spread across several networks.

## What a unified inbox changes

The principle is simple: one list of conversations, whichever network they came from. But it’s what you build around it that matters:

1. **A single queue.** Direct messages and comments side by side, with the network shown.
2. **Shared statuses.** Open, pending, snoozed, closed: the whole team sees where each conversation stands.
3. **An owner.** An assigned conversation has someone responsible for it.
4. **An eye on deadlines.** On Messenger and Instagram, the reply window is limited in time (see [the 24-hour window](/blog/messenger-instagram-24-hour-window)): the inbox should show what is about to expire.

## What to ask of a unified inbox tool

- That it keeps the **context of the conversation**, not just the latest message.
- That it lets you **reply without switching apps**, from the conversation itself.
- That it’s **clear about what it doesn’t do**. Each network limits what third-party tools can read or send; a good tool says so instead of promising what doesn’t exist.

> [!NOTE]
> Postbiz shows the real status of each network on [the Channels page](/canaux), on three levels: beta, soon, not available. Messenger and Instagram private messages and Facebook, Instagram and YouTube comments are in beta; other networks are coming. Some networks don’t open their private messages to third-party tools, and we can’t work around that.
