When a customer writes to you on Messenger or Instagram, a clock starts. Meta lets a business reply freely for **24 hours** after the customer’s last message. After that, the conversation closes: sending a message is refused, except in the special cases Meta provides for.

## What the rule says

The window reopens every time the customer writes again. It doesn’t depend on who replied: until the customer writes, the countdown keeps running.

- **Inside the window:** you can send free-form messages (text, images, links).
- **Outside the window:** sending is refused by default. Meta provides a few exceptions, such as a tag reserved for a human answering a support request, which extends the delay to 7 days.
- **Public comments:** to reply privately to a comment, Meta allows one message within 7 days of the comment.

> [!NOTE]
> These rules come from Meta’s documentation at the time of writing and may change. Check [Meta’s official documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform) before building a process on them.

## Why it’s a team problem

A 24-hour rule is easy for one person to follow. With several people, it gets much harder:

- nobody knows which conversation expires first;
- a “I’ll look at it tomorrow” can use up the window without solving anything;
- weekends and public holidays eat part of the delay.

## Three habits that keep conversations from slipping away

1. **Sort by deadline, not by age.** The oldest conversation isn’t necessarily the most urgent: the one that expires in two hours is.
2. **Reply short, right away.** A useful first message (“Got it, here’s the link…”) beats a perfect answer that arrives too late.
3. **Share the conversations out.** A conversation assigned to someone has an owner; a “shared” conversation doesn’t.

> [!TIP]
> Set a handover time. If someone finishes at 6 pm, they pass every conversation that expires before noon the next day to the next person, with one line of context.
