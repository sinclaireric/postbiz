Articles promising “the best time to post” are tempting. The problem: they average millions of accounts that look nothing like yours. Your audience has its own habits, and that’s what you need to watch.

## Why averages mislead

- An audience of professionals isn’t online at the same hours as an audience of students.
- An audience spread across several time zones has no shared “evening”.
- Each network’s algorithm changes: what held last year may not hold today.

## A four-step method

1. **Start from what you know.** When do your customers write to you? What time do messages and comments arrive? Your inbox is already a presence indicator.
2. **Pick two or three slots to test**, not ten. For example: early morning, lunch break, end of the day.
3. **Post the same type of content in each slot** for three to four weeks. Comparing different content proves nothing.
4. **Compare, keep, repeat.** Keep the slot that works best, replace the weakest with a new candidate.

> [!TIP]
> Log your tests in a very simple table: date, network, slot, type of content, result. After a month you’ll have your own data instead of other people’s.

## What matters as much as the time

- **Consistency.** An audience gets used to finding you at a given moment.
- **Being around after you post.** The first replies to a post count: if you publish when you’re there to answer, the conversation starts.
- **Time zone.** Check your calendar’s before you schedule.
